How to choose

Capacity
If you usually cook about half the maximum capacity, a size smaller cooks more evenly.
Heating method
Microcomputer models heat with an element at the bottom and are cheap. IH heats the whole pot and is more powerful. Pressure IH cooks at a higher temperature under pressure, which tends to give chewier rice.
Inner pot
Thick pots and materials such as clay, iron or copper change how well heat is held. Most of the price difference lies here.
Cleaning
The fewer parts you have to wash every time (inner lid, steam vent), the easier it is to keep up.
Keep warm
Keeping rice warm for a long time tends to yellow and dry it. If you freeze your rice, prioritise how it cooks over how it keeps warm.

Watch out for

  • A damaged inner-pot coating costs money to replace. Check the price of a replacement inner pot too.
  • Using it outside Japan: Made for Japan’s 100 V mains, and the manufacturer supports use in Japan only; never use it on 220–240 V. Japanese makers also sell separate models made for overseas voltages.

How the ranking works

For each category, up to 60 Rakuten listings are fetched in order of review count and filtered by excluded words, price range and a minimum number of reviews. Listings of the same product by different shops (same model number, or the same specs and a near-identical title) are merged into the best-rated one, with the other shops’ prices kept in other_offers. The rest are ranked by a Bayesian average rating.

score = (C × m + n × r) / (C + n) … r = the product’s average rating, n = number of reviews, m = average rating of all candidates, C = 50

So that a 4.4★ with thousands of reviews ranks above a 5★ with a handful. The fewer the reviews, the more a rating is pulled towards the average.

Specs are extracted mechanically from product titles and may be wrong. Check the product page before buying.
