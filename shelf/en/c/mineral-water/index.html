How to choose

Price per L
Pack sizes differ (2 L × 6, 2 L × 9, 500 ml × 24 and so on), so divide by the total volume. 2 L bottles are generally cheaper than 500 ml.
Hardness
In Japan, water under about 100 mg/L is usually called soft, 100–300 medium-hard and over 300 hard. Soft water suits Japanese tea and dashi.
Shipping
Water is heavy, and with shipping extra the price difference easily reverses. Compare listings with shipping included.
Emergency stock
For disaster preparedness, “rolling stock” (drinking it and buying more as you go) is recommended. There is also long-life emergency water.

Watch out for

  • Label-less bottles are easier to sort for recycling, but details such as hardness may be printed only on the box.
  • Using it outside Japan: No special issue, but it is heavy and bulky, so it is mostly useful for people living in Japan.

How the ranking works

For each category, up to 60 Rakuten listings are fetched in order of review count and filtered by excluded words, price range and a minimum number of reviews. Listings of the same product by different shops (same model number, or the same specs and a near-identical title) are merged into the best-rated one, with the other shops’ prices kept in other_offers. The rest are ranked by a Bayesian average rating.

score = (C × m + n × r) / (C + n) … r = the product’s average rating, n = number of reviews, m = average rating of all candidates, C = 50

So that a 4.4★ with thousands of reviews ranks above a 5★ with a handful. The fewer the reviews, the more a rating is pulled towards the average.

Specs are extracted mechanically from product titles and may be wrong. Check the product page before buying.
