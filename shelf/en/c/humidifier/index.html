How to choose

Steam
Boils the water, so it is hygienic and humidifies strongly. Uses a few hundred watts, and some models have a hot outlet.
Evaporative
Blows air through a wet filter: low power and quiet. Output depends on room temperature and humidity, and the filter needs regular cleaning and replacement.
Ultrasonic
Cheap, small and quiet. It turns the water straight into mist, so a neglected tank spreads bacteria, and minerals in tap water can leave white dust.
Hybrid
Adds a heater to an evaporative or ultrasonic design to balance output and power use. More expensive.
Room size
Two figures are given, for a wooden Japanese-style room and a prefab Western-style room; the wooden-room figure is smaller. Compare using the one that matches your home.

Watch out for

  • Check the price and availability of replacement filters (running cost) first.
  • A tank with a narrow opening is hard to clean, which matters for hygiene.
  • Using it outside Japan: Made for Japan’s 100 V mains, and the manufacturer supports use in Japan only. 110–120 V is above its rating and can cause overheating or failure; never use it on 220–240 V.

How the ranking works

For each category, up to 60 Rakuten listings are fetched in order of review count and filtered by excluded words, price range and a minimum number of reviews. Listings of the same product by different shops (same model number, or the same specs and a near-identical title) are merged into the best-rated one, with the other shops’ prices kept in other_offers. The rest are ranked by a Bayesian average rating.

score = (C × m + n × r) / (C + n) … r = the product’s average rating, n = number of reviews, m = average rating of all candidates, C = 50

So that a 4.4★ with thousands of reviews ranks above a 5★ with a handful. The fewer the reviews, the more a rating is pulled towards the average.

Specs are extracted mechanically from product titles and may be wrong. Check the product page before buying.
