How to choose

Output (W)
Fast-charging an iPhone needs 20 W or more. For a laptop, match or exceed the wattage of its own adapter (for example 65 W or 96 W).
Sharing between ports
A “65 W total” charger may split into 45 W + 20 W with two devices. If you charge a laptop and a phone together, check the split.
PPS
“Super fast charging” on Galaxy and similar phones needs a charger that supports USB PD PPS.
GaN
Gallium nitride chargers are smaller and lighter for the same output. Worth it if you carry one.
Cable
Above 60 W you need a 5 A (e-marked) USB-C cable. With a 3 A cable even a powerful charger stops at 60 W.

Watch out for

  • Chargers without the PSE mark cannot be sold in Japan. Check for it.
  • Comparing only the total output can hide a single port that is too weak.
  • Using it outside Japan: Most USB-C chargers sold in Japan accept 100–240 V input and work abroad with a plug adapter (Japan uses Type A, two flat pins). Check for “100-240V” on the label.

How the ranking works

For each category, up to 60 Rakuten listings are fetched in order of review count and filtered by excluded words, price range and a minimum number of reviews. Listings of the same product by different shops (same model number, or the same specs and a near-identical title) are merged into the best-rated one, with the other shops’ prices kept in other_offers. The rest are ranked by a Bayesian average rating.

score = (C × m + n × r) / (C + n) … r = the product’s average rating, n = number of reviews, m = average rating of all candidates, C = 50

So that a 4.4★ with thousands of reviews ranks above a 5★ with a handful. The fewer the reviews, the more a rating is pulled towards the average.

Specs are extracted mechanically from product titles and may be wrong. Check the product page before buying.
