How to choose

Price per battery
Pack sizes differ (4, 20, 40 and so on), so divide by the number of batteries.
Recommended use-by period
A guide to how long they keep from manufacture. For emergency stock, choose a long period and use them up and replace them within it.
Use
High-drain devices (game controllers, camera flashes and the like) run batteries down fast. If you change them often, rechargeable batteries can be cheaper in the long run.
Leaks
Removing dead batteries, and batteries from devices you will not use for a long time, prevents damage from leaks.

Watch out for

  • Do not mix new and old batteries, or batteries of different types.
  • When throwing them away, cover the terminals with tape and follow your municipality’s sorting rules.
  • Using it outside Japan: Work in any AA device. Alkaline batteries can go in your luggage; follow your airline’s rules.

How the ranking works

For each category, up to 60 Rakuten listings are fetched in order of review count and filtered by excluded words, price range and a minimum number of reviews. Listings of the same product by different shops (same model number, or the same specs and a near-identical title) are merged into the best-rated one, with the other shops’ prices kept in other_offers. The rest are ranked by a Bayesian average rating.

score = (C × m + n × r) / (C + n) … r = the product’s average rating, n = number of reviews, m = average rating of all candidates, C = 50

So that a 4.4★ with thousands of reviews ranks above a 5★ with a handful. The fewer the reviews, the more a rating is pulled towards the average.

Specs are extracted mechanically from product titles and may be wrong. Check the product page before buying.
